DASH is not for everyone yet. This page says where it is a good fit, where other browsers are ahead, and what to check in any browser you use.

Updated {{updated}}. About DASH only: I do not make claims about other browsers' settings because they change.

Where DASH is a good fit

  • You want nothing sent about you. No telemetry, no account, and no analytics in the app or on this website. Every connection DASH makes is listed in the privacy policy.
  • You want your data on your device. History, bookmarks and settings are stored locally.
  • You want to read the code. DASH is open source under GPL-3.0 and you can verify what you download.
  • You use Windows 10 or 11, or Android.

Where other browsers are ahead today

  • Platforms. DASH is not on macOS, iOS or Linux yet.
  • Extensions. Big browsers have large extension stores. DASH has built-in tools instead.
  • Sync across devices. DASH keeps data local, so there is no cloud sync.
  • Track record. DASH is made by one developer. Large teams and years of public scrutiny are a real advantage in security.
  • Signed installer. The Windows build is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen warns. The code signing policy describes the plan.

What to check in any browser

Before you trust a browser with your browsing, look for these in its own settings and documentation:

  1. Does it send usage or crash data by default, and can you switch that off?
  2. Does it ask you to sign in, and what stops working if you do not?
  3. Is the full source code public, and can you build it yourself?
  4. Are ads and trackers blocked by default, or only after you change something?
  5. Where is your history stored, and does it leave your device?

If DASH ever fails one of these, please open an issue.
